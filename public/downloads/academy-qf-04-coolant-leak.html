Find and Fix a Coolant Leak

Track down where your coolant is going and fix the most common leaks yourself.

Time: 30 minutes to 2 hoursLevel: Beginner to intermediateSteps: 5

Losing coolant leads to overheating, and overheating can destroy an engine. The good news: many leaks are a loose clamp, a cracked hose, or a worn radiator cap, all cheap fixes. This guide shows you how to find the source and fix it right.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Never open the radiator cap or coolant reservoir cap on a hot engine. The system is under pressure and can spray scalding coolant. Wait until the engine is cool to the touch.
  • Coolant is toxic and tastes sweet to pets and children. Clean up spills right away and store it sealed.
  • Dispose of old coolant at a parts store or hazardous waste site, not down a drain.
  • Keep hands clear of the fan, which can run with the engine off.

Step 1Is it really coolant?

  1. Coolant is usually green, orange, pink, yellow or blue, and smells sweet.
  2. Clear water dripping near the front passenger side after running the AC is normal condensation.
  3. Brown or black and slick is oil. Red and oily is often transmission or power steering fluid.
  4. Check the reservoir level against the MIN and MAX marks, cold, and write it down. Check again after a few days of driving.

Step 2Find the leak

  1. Slide cardboard under the engine overnight. The drip spot shows roughly where to look.
  2. With the engine cool, use a flashlight to check: hoses and clamps (wet spots, crusty residue, soft or swollen hoses), the radiator (seams where plastic tanks meet metal core), the water pump (a small weep hole that drips when the seal fails), the thermostat housing, and the reservoir for cracks.
  3. Dried coolant leaves a white, green or pink crust. Follow it uphill to the source.
  4. Inside the car: a wet passenger floor, a sweet smell, or foggy windows with the heat on point to the heater core.

Step 3Pressure test (finds hidden leaks)

  1. With the engine cold, remove the cap and attach the tester adapter that fits your radiator or reservoir neck.
  2. Pump to the pressure stamped on your cap (often 13 to 16 psi). Do not go over it.
  3. Watch the gauge for 15 minutes. If it drops, look and listen for the leak while it's pressurized.
  4. Test the cap too with the cap adapter. A cap that won't hold pressure lets coolant boil out.
Pro tip: Add UV dye to the coolant, drive a day, then scan the engine with a UV light. The leak glows bright.

Step 4Fix common leaks

  1. Loose clamp: tighten it, or replace a weak spring clamp with a new one.
  2. Bad hose: drain coolant below the hose level into a pan, loosen the clamps, twist the hose to break it free, install the new hose all the way on, and position the clamps just behind the bead.
  3. Bad cap: replace it with one with the same pressure rating.
  4. Cracked radiator, leaking water pump, or thermostat housing: these are bigger jobs. Get the part and look up a guide for your vehicle, or have a shop do it.
Caution: Avoid stop-leak products except as a roadside emergency. They can clog the radiator and heater core.

Step 5Refill and bleed the air out

  1. Use the coolant type listed in your owner's manual. Mixing types can cause problems.
  2. Fill slowly to the correct level. A spill-free funnel attached to the radiator neck helps a lot.
  3. Set the heater to max hot and fan low. Start the engine with the funnel in place and let it idle.
  4. Watch for bubbles rising. Squeeze the upper radiator hose gently (with gloves; it'll warm up) to push air out. Some engines have bleeder screws. Check yours.
  5. When the thermostat opens (upper hose gets hot) and bubbles stop, shut it off, let it cool, and top off. Recheck the level cold over the next few days.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Milky oil on the dipstick or cap
  • Steady white, sweet-smelling exhaust
  • Bubbles constantly rising in the reservoir with the engine running
  • Heater core or water pump replacement on engines where it's buried
  • Overheating that continues after fixing the leak
