Replace the Engine and Cabin Air Filters

The two filters shops love to upsell, usually tool-free, often under $40 for both.

Time: 10 to 20 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

The engine air filter keeps dirt out of the engine. The cabin filter cleans the air you breathe and is behind most weak-airflow and musty-smell complaints. Both are easy to see, easy to judge, and easy to replace.

A shop or dealer charges
$80 to $150 for both
You'll spend
$20 to $40

Safety first, read before you start

  • Engine off, keys out of the ignition.
  • Don't let anything fall into the open air intake. Cover it with a clean rag while the filter is out.
  • Wear a dust mask if the old cabin filter is full of leaves or mold.

What you'll learn

  • Find and open both filter housings
  • Judge whether a filter actually needs replacing
  • Install filters the right way round
  • Know when a shop's 'your filter is dirty' is true

What you need

  • Correct engine air filter and cabin filter for your car
  • Screwdriver or socket set (some housings use screws)
  • Flashlight
  • Shop vacuum or rag

Step 1Check the engine air filter

  1. Open the hood and find the black plastic box on the intake tube, usually near the front or side of the engine.
  2. Release the clips or screws and lift the lid.
  3. Hold the filter up to a light. If light barely passes through, or it's caked with dirt, leaves or bugs, replace it.
  4. Wipe or vacuum loose debris out of the box, keeping it away from the intake opening.

Step 2Install the new engine filter

  1. Match the new filter to the old one: same shape, rubber seal facing the same way.
  2. Seat it fully so the rubber rim sits flat all the way around.
  3. Close the lid and fasten every clip or screw. A gap lets unfiltered air into the engine.
Caution: If you disconnected a sensor (some boxes have a mass airflow sensor plug), reconnect it, or you'll get a check engine light.

Step 3Find the cabin filter

  1. On most cars it's behind the glovebox. Empty the glovebox and look for side stops or a damper arm.
  2. Squeeze the sides or unclip the arm so the glovebox swings down past its stops.
  3. Unclip the rectangular cover behind it and slide the old filter out, noting the airflow arrow.
Pro tip: Search your exact model plus 'cabin filter' for a 60-second video before you start. Some are under the hood near the windshield instead.

Step 4Install the new cabin filter

  1. Point the airflow arrow the same way as the old one (usually down or toward the rear).
  2. Slide it in without crushing the pleats, refit the cover and the glovebox.
  3. Run the fan on high for a minute. Airflow should be stronger and any musty smell weaker.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Check engine light after the swapA sensor plug or intake clamp was left loose. Reseat it and clear the code.
Glovebox won't closeA stop or the damper arm isn't back in place. Reseat both sides.
Still musty after a new cabin filterRun AC off with fan on high for a few minutes before shutting the car off to dry the evaporator.

When to stop and call a pro

  • The air box is cracked or a clamp is broken
  • Water or oil is in the air box
  • The cabin filter is behind the dash and needs panels removed you're not comfortable with

Printable checklist

  • Right filters for your car
  • Engine filter seated, seal flat
  • All clips and screws fastened
  • Sensor plugs reconnected
  • Cabin filter arrow correct
  • Glovebox stops reattached
  • Fan tested on high
