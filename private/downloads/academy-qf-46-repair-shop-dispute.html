Win a Dispute With a Car Repair Shop

Use estimates, authorizations and warranties to get a failed or overbilled repair made right.

Time: 1 to 3 hoursLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Many states require written estimates and your authorization before work beyond the estimate. Repairs usually carry a parts and labor warranty. A clear paper trail turns a shouting match into a quick fix.

Read this first

  • A shop can often keep your car until authorized work is paid (a mechanic's lien). If you dispute, you may need to pay under protest to get the car back, then pursue a refund.
  • Don't let anyone else fix the problem before the original shop has a chance under warranty, unless it's unsafe to drive.
  • Look up your state's auto repair rules; many states have a consumer protection office for auto repair.

What you need

  • Written estimate
  • Invoice
  • Any texts or notes of approvals
  • Old parts if returned
  • Photos

Step 1Check the paperwork

  1. Compare the invoice to the estimate. Was extra work approved, and how (in writing, by phone)?
  2. Ask for your old parts; many states give you that right if requested at drop-off.

Step 2Use the warranty

  1. If the repair failed, return to the shop promptly and ask for the warranty repair in writing.
  2. Ask the shop to put its diagnosis in writing.

Step 3Dispute charges

  1. Write a short letter listing unauthorized charges and asking for a refund.
  2. If you must pay to get the car, write 'paid under protest' on the receipt or in an email the same day.
  3. If you paid by credit card, consider a card dispute for unauthorized work.

Step 4Escalate

  1. File a complaint with your state's attorney general or auto repair bureau.
  2. Small claims court with your estimate, invoice and a second shop's written opinion.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Shop says you approved by phoneAsk for their record of the call (time, person). Many states require documented authorization.
Second shop finds the repair wasn't doneGet that in writing with photos.

Get help if

  • Unsafe repairs (brakes, steering)
  • Large amounts or fraud
  • Your state's auto repair regulator

Printable checklist

  • Estimate vs invoice compared
  • Old parts requested
  • Warranty repair requested in writing
  • Paid under protest documented
  • Complaint filed
  • Second opinion in writing
