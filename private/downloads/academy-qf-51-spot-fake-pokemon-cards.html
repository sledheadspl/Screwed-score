Spot Fake Pokémon Cards in 60 Seconds

Check print, color, texture, light and edges so you never buy a counterfeit.

Time: 15 minutes to learn, 60 seconds per cardLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Counterfeit cards are everywhere online and at some tables. The good news: most fakes fail simple checks. Always compare against a card you know is real from the same set, or official high-resolution images.

Buying one fake chase card can cost
$20 to $500+
You'll spend
$0 to $15 (jeweler's loupe)

Read this first

  • Never use a destructive test (ripping, burning) on a card you might want to keep or return.
  • If buying online, insist on clear photos of front, back and edges, and use a payment method with buyer protection.

What you'll learn

  • The quick visual checks
  • Font, symbols and text errors
  • The light and edge checks

What you need

  • A known-real card from the same era
  • Phone flashlight
  • Jeweler's loupe (optional)

Step 1Look at the front

  1. Compare the font, spacing and attack text to a real card. Fakes often have the wrong font weight or misspellings.
  2. Check energy symbols: fakes are often off-center, the wrong size or the wrong shade.
  3. Holo pattern: compare the shimmer pattern to a real card from the same set; fakes are often flat, too rainbow or patterned wrong.
  4. Check the set symbol, card number and copyright line at the bottom.

Step 2Look at the back

  1. The blue on the back should match a real card. Fakes are often too light, too purple or blotchy.
  2. Check the swirl detail and the Poké Ball edge for blurriness.

Step 3Feel and light

  1. Real cards have a particular stiffness and finish; many fakes feel too glossy, too thin or waxy.
  2. Shine a phone flashlight behind the card next to a real one. Many fakes let noticeably more light through.
  3. Look at the edge with a loupe. Many genuine cards show a thin dark layer in the middle of the card stock that fakes lack.

Step 4If you got a fake

  1. Message the seller with photos and request a refund.
  2. Open a case with the marketplace or payment provider (item not as described).
  3. Report the listing.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Card passes most checks but feels offCompare with several real cards from the same set; print runs vary slightly, so look for multiple failures, not one.
Graded slab looks offSee the scams lesson: verify the certificate number on the grading company's site.

Get a second opinion if

  • High-value cards: ask a reputable local card shop or authenticated grading
  • The seller pushes you to pay outside the platform

Printable checklist

  • Font and text compared
  • Energy symbols checked
  • Holo pattern compared
  • Back color checked
  • Light test vs real card
  • Edge checked with loupe
  • Paid with buyer protection
