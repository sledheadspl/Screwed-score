Small Claims Court, Start to Finish

File, serve, prepare and present a small claims case, and collect what you win.

Time: A few hours spread over weeksLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Small claims court is built for people without lawyers. Limits vary by state (often several thousand dollars up to $10,000 or more). A clear story, organized evidence and a demand letter first are what make cases succeed.

Hiring a lawyer for a small dispute
Often more than the claim
You'll spend
$30 to $100 filing fee (often recoverable if you win)

Read this first

  • Check your court's limit, fees and rules on its website.
  • Sue the correct legal name of the business (check your state's business registry).
  • Missing your court date usually means losing. Put it on your calendar.

What you need

  • Contracts, receipts, photos, texts and emails
  • The other party's legal name and address
  • Demand letter copy

Step 1Demand letter

  1. Explain what happened, the amount owed, and give a deadline (for example 14 days) before you file.
  2. Many disputes settle here.

Step 2File and serve

  1. File in the correct court (usually where the defendant is or where the deal happened).
  2. Pay the fee or ask about a fee waiver.
  3. Serve the defendant using an approved method (often sheriff, process server or certified mail).

Step 3Prepare

  1. Write a one-page timeline.
  2. Organize evidence in order with copies for the judge and the other side.
  3. Bring witnesses or written statements if allowed.

Step 4Court day and collecting

  1. Arrive early, be brief and factual, and answer the judge's questions directly.
  2. If you win and they don't pay, ask the clerk about collection tools (wage garnishment or bank levy, depending on your state).

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this

They countersuePrepare to address their claim with your evidence too.

Get help if

  • The amount is above the limit
  • Complex contracts or many parties
  • Many courts offer free small claims advisors

Printable checklist

  • Demand letter sent
  • Correct court and defendant name
  • Filed and served
  • Timeline and evidence copies
  • Court date on calendar
  • Collection steps if needed
