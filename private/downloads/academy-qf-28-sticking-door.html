Fix a Sticking or Sagging Door

Stop rubbing, dragging and latches that won't catch without removing the door.

Time: 20 to 60 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 3

A door that rubs at the top corner or won't latch has usually sagged on its hinges. Tightening hinge screws or driving one long screw into the framing pulls it back into line, often without any planing.

What you'll learn

  • Find where the door rubs
  • Tighten and lengthen hinge screws
  • Adjust the strike plate
  • Know when a little planing is the answer

What you need

  • Screwdriver or drill
  • 3" wood screws
  • Pencil or paper to find rub points
  • Chisel or file (for the strike)

Step 1Find the rub

  1. Look for worn paint on the door edge or frame.
  2. Slide paper around the closed door; where it binds is the rub point.

Step 2Tighten the hinges

  1. Tighten every hinge screw. Stripped holes won't hold.
  2. Replace one screw in the top hinge (the one nearest the doorstop) with a 3-inch screw that reaches the wall framing. It pulls the door up and back.
Pro tip: If a rub is at the top latch side, the top hinge is usually the culprit.

Step 3Fix the latch

  1. If the latch misses the strike, file the strike opening slightly or move the strike plate up or down.
  2. Seasonal swelling? Wait for dry weather before planing; it may fix itself.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Screws spin in the holesGlue in wooden toothpicks or golf tees, cut flush, and redrive the screws.
Still rubs after hinge fixPlane or sand the rub point lightly, then seal the bare wood.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Cracked frame or structural settling
  • Exterior door that won't seal after adjustment

Printable checklist

  • Rub point found
  • All hinge screws tight
  • Long screw in top hinge
  • Strike aligned
  • Bare wood sealed if planed
