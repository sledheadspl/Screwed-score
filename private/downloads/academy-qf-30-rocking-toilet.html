Fix a Rocking or Leaking-at-the-Base Toilet

Tighten a loose toilet, or replace the wax ring the right way.

Time: 1 to 2 hoursLevel: IntermediateSteps: 3

A toilet that rocks or leaks at the base can rot the subfloor. Sometimes tightening the bolts is enough. If water seeps out when flushing, the wax ring needs replacing, which is a heavy but straightforward job.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Toilets are heavy and porcelain cracks if dropped or overtightened. Lift with your legs and have help if needed.
  • Wear gloves; plug the open drain with a rag to stop sewer gas and dropped parts.
  • If the floor around it is soft or spongy, stop. The subfloor needs repair.

What you'll learn

  • Diagnose rocking versus leaking
  • Tighten closet bolts without cracking porcelain
  • Pull and reset a toilet with a new wax ring

What you need

  • Wax ring (or wax-free seal) and new closet bolts
  • Adjustable wrench
  • Putty knife
  • Sponge and bucket
  • Plastic shims
  • Rag

Step 1Diagnose

  1. Rocking, no water: tighten bolts, maybe shim.
  2. Water at the base after flushing, or a sewage smell: replace the wax ring.

Step 2Tighten (if only rocking)

  1. Pop off the bolt caps. Tighten each nut a little at a time, alternating sides.
  2. Stop as soon as it's firm. Overtightening cracks the base.
  3. Use shims under the base for uneven floors, then caulk around (leave a small gap at the back so leaks show).

Step 3Replace the wax ring

  1. Turn off the water, flush, and sponge out the remaining water.
  2. Disconnect the supply line, remove the nuts, and lift the toilet straight up onto cardboard.
  3. Scrape off the old wax from the flange and toilet horn. Plug the drain with a rag.
  4. Fit new bolts in the flange slots, press the new ring onto the toilet horn (or flange), remove the rag.
  5. Lower the toilet straight down over the bolts in one go, press down firmly, and tighten nuts alternately.
  6. Reconnect water, flush several times and check for leaks.
Caution: Don't rock the toilet after setting it on a wax ring; it breaks the seal.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Still leaks after new ringFlange too low or broken. A thicker ring or flange extender/repair may be needed.
Base crackedReplace the toilet; don't try to seal a cracked base.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Soft or rotted floor
  • Broken or corroded flange
  • Cast iron drain issues

Printable checklist

  • Water off and drained
  • Old wax fully removed
  • New bolts in flange
  • Drain plugged while working, unplugged before setting
  • Set straight down
  • Nuts alternated, not overtightened
  • No leaks after several flushes
