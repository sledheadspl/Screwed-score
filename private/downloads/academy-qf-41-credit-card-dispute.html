Dispute a Credit Card Charge the Right Way

Get your money back for billing errors, double charges and things you never received.

Time: 20 to 40 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

For credit cards, the federal Fair Credit Billing Act gives you dispute rights for billing errors, including charges for goods or services you didn't receive or didn't accept. Doing it in writing, on time, with evidence is what wins.

Read this first

  • Generally you must send a written dispute so the issuer receives it within 60 days after the statement with the error was sent to you. Don't wait.
  • Try the merchant first and keep proof you did (many issuers ask).
  • Debit card disputes have different, often weaker, protections; act even faster.

What you need

  • The statement showing the charge
  • Receipts, order confirmations, photos, emails with the merchant
  • Tracking or proof of non-delivery or return

Step 1Does it qualify?

  1. Charge you didn't make or authorize.
  2. Wrong amount or a double charge.
  3. Goods or services not delivered, or not as agreed.
  4. A return or cancellation the merchant didn't credit.

Step 2Try the merchant

  1. Contact the merchant in writing asking for a refund, with your order number.
  2. Give them a short deadline (for example 7 days).

Step 3File with your card issuer

  1. Use the issuer's dispute form or send a letter to the billing-error address on your statement (not the payment address).
  2. Include your name, account number, the charge, the amount and why it's wrong.
  3. Attach evidence. Keep copies.
  4. You can generally withhold payment of the disputed amount (not the rest of your bill) while it's investigated.
Pro tip: Write it like a short timeline: what you bought, what happened, what you did to fix it, what you want.

Step 4After you file

  1. The issuer must acknowledge your dispute within 30 days and resolve it within two billing cycles (no more than 90 days).
  2. Respond quickly if they ask for more information.
  3. If denied, ask for the documents they relied on and respond with more evidence.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Merchant offers store credit insteadYou can decline and continue the dispute if you want a refund.
Dispute deniedAsk for the reason and evidence, add yours, and request reconsideration; complain to the CFPB if mishandled.

Escalate if

  • Issuer mishandles the dispute: CFPB complaint (consumerfinance.gov/complaint)
  • Large amounts or fraud patterns

Printable checklist

  • Qualifying reason identified
  • Merchant contacted in writing
  • Within 60 days
  • Sent to billing-error address
  • Evidence attached
  • Disputed amount withheld only
  • Follow-ups answered
