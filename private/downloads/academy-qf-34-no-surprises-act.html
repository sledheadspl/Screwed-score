Surprise Medical Bills: Use the No Surprises Act

Know when surprise out-of-network bills are banned and how to use a good faith estimate.

Time: 30 to 60 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 3

Since 2022 the federal No Surprises Act protects most people with private insurance from surprise out-of-network bills for emergency care, for certain out-of-network providers at in-network facilities (like an anesthesiologist at your in-network hospital), and for air ambulances. Uninsured and self-pay patients have a right to a good faith estimate before scheduled care.

Step 1Is your bill protected?

  1. Emergency care at any hospital ER, in or out of network: generally protected.
  2. Care at an in-network hospital or surgery center by an out-of-network doctor you didn't choose: generally protected.
  3. Air ambulance: protected.
  4. In these cases you should owe only your in-network cost sharing.

Step 2Fight a balance bill

  1. Write to the provider stating that the bill appears to violate the No Surprises Act, and that you'll pay only your in-network cost share per your EOB.
  2. Send a copy to your insurer.
  3. If they won't fix it, file a complaint through the federal No Surprises help line or website (cms.gov/nosurprises).

Step 3Self-pay: get a good faith estimate

  1. When scheduling care without insurance (or not using it), ask for a good faith estimate in writing.
  2. If the final bill is at least $400 more than the estimate from that provider, you can start a patient-provider dispute through the federal process.
  3. You must start it within 120 calendar days of the bill date.
Caution: Check the current rules and dollar thresholds at cms.gov/nosurprises before filing; they can be updated.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Provider says the law doesn't applyAsk which exception they're claiming, in writing, and file a complaint if you disagree.
Ground ambulance billCheck your state's rules; many states have added ambulance protections.

Get extra help if

  • The provider sends you to collections
  • The bill is large and you're unsure which rules apply
  • Your state insurance department can help

  • Compared bill to EOB in-network share
  • Letter citing the Act sent
  • Insurer copied
