Find a Blown Fuse or Bad Relay

Track down why something electrical stopped working before paying for a diagnosis.

Time: 15 to 45 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 3

When one thing quits all at once (radio, power outlet, a pair of lights) it's very often a fuse. Fuses protect wiring, and a relay is a switch that lets a small current control a big one. Both are cheap and easy to test.

A shop charges
$100 to $180 diagnostic fee
You'll spend
$5 to $25

Safety first, read before you start

  • Turn the ignition and the circuit off before pulling fuses.
  • Always replace a fuse with the same amp rating. A bigger fuse can melt wiring and start a fire.
  • If a new fuse blows right away, stop. There's a short to find, and replacing fuses won't fix it.

What you'll learn

  • Find the right fuse box and fuse
  • Test fuses by eye and with a meter
  • Swap-test a relay
  • Know when it's a short, not a fuse

What you need

  • Owner's manual (fuse diagrams)
  • Fuse puller (often in the fuse box lid)
  • Assorted replacement fuses of the right type
  • Test light or multimeter
  • Flashlight

Step 1Find the fuse

  1. Most cars have one fuse box under the hood and one inside (under the dash, side of the dash, or in the kick panel).
  2. Use the diagram on the lid or in the manual to find the fuse for the dead item.
  3. Pull it straight out with the puller.

Step 2Test it

  1. Hold it to light. A broken metal strip inside means it's blown.
  2. With a test light or meter: with the circuit on, touch both small test points on top of the fuse in the box. Power on both sides means the fuse is good.
  3. Replace a blown fuse with the same amperage and type, then test the item.
Pro tip: Many fuse boxes hold spares. Replace any spare you use so it's there next time.

Step 3Swap-test a relay

  1. Find the relay for the circuit (fan, horn, fuel pump, headlights) on the diagram.
  2. Find an identical relay (same part number) for a non-critical circuit, like the horn.
  3. Swap them. If the dead item now works and the horn stops, the relay was bad. Buy one and put both back.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
New fuse blows instantlyThere's a short circuit. Stop and have the wiring checked.
Fuse good, item still deadCould be the switch, a connector, ground, or the item itself. That's a diagnosis job.
Fuse blows only sometimesIntermittent short, often a pinched wire in a door hinge or trunk lid.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Repeatedly blowing fuses
  • Burning smell, melted fuse box or wiring
  • Airbag, ABS or engine-management circuits (leave those to a pro)

Printable checklist

  • Circuit and ignition off
  • Right fuse box and fuse found
  • Fuse tested
  • Replaced with same amp rating
  • Relay swap-tested if needed
  • Spare replaced
