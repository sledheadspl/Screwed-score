Get Your Security Deposit Back

Protect your deposit at move-in and move-out, and demand it back when a landlord keeps it.

Time: 1 to 2 hours across the leaseLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

State law sets how long a landlord has to return your deposit (often 14 to 30 days) and generally requires an itemized list of deductions. Normal wear and tear usually can't be charged. Photos and a forwarding address are your best tools.

A lost deposit is often
A full month's rent
You'll spend
$0 (small claims filing fee if needed)

Read this first

  • Look up your state's deposit law for the exact deadline and rules.
  • Give your forwarding address in writing.
  • Don't withhold your last month's rent 'as the deposit' unless your state allows it.

What you need

  • Lease
  • Move-in checklist and photos
  • Move-out photos and video
  • Forwarding address

Step 1At move-in

  1. Take dated photos and video of every room, including floors, walls, appliances and fixtures.
  2. Note every existing defect on a checklist and send a copy to the landlord.

Step 2At move-out

  1. Clean thoroughly and repair damage you caused.
  2. Take the same photos again, room by room.
  3. Return keys and give your forwarding address in writing.

Step 3Wear and tear vs damage

  1. Normal wear: faded paint, minor scuffs, worn carpet from normal use, small nail holes (in many places).
  2. Damage: large holes, stains, broken fixtures, pet damage.

Step 4Demand it back

  1. If the deadline passes or deductions look wrong, send a demand letter citing your state's deposit law, the amount owed and a deadline.
  2. Attach photos. Keep a copy.
  3. If unpaid, small claims court is the next step (see the small claims lesson); some states award extra damages for bad-faith withholding.
