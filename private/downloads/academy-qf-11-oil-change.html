Change Your Own Oil and Filter

Do the most common service yourself, with the right oil, the right filter and zero mess.

Time: 30 to 45 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

An oil change is draining old oil, swapping the filter and refilling with the oil your owner's manual specifies. Shops make their money on the upsells, not the oil. Do it yourself and you also get a free look under the car every few months.

A quick-lube shop charges
$60 to $120
You'll spend
$30 to $50 (oil and filter)

Safety first, read before you start

  • Never get under a car held up only by a jack. Use ramps or rated jack stands on solid, level ground, and chock the wheels.
  • Hot oil burns. Let the engine cool for at least 20 to 30 minutes so the oil is warm, not hot.
  • Wear safety glasses and nitrile gloves. Used oil contains contaminants you shouldn't keep on your skin.
  • Used oil and filters are hazardous waste. Never pour them on the ground or down a drain; most auto parts stores take them free.

What you'll learn

  • Find the right oil type, weight and amount for your car
  • Drain the oil and change the filter without leaks
  • Refill and check the level correctly
  • Reset the oil-life reminder
  • Spot leaks and problems while you're under there

What you need

  • Correct oil (check the owner's manual: weight like 0W-20 and the spec it must meet)
  • Matching oil filter (and a new drain-plug washer if your car uses one)
  • Socket or wrench for the drain plug
  • Oil filter wrench
  • Drain pan (8 quart or larger), funnel, rags
  • Ramps or jack and jack stands
  • Gloves and safety glasses

Step 1Get the right parts

  1. Look up the oil weight (for example 0W-20), the spec (for example API SP or a manufacturer approval) and the capacity with filter in your owner's manual.
  2. Buy the filter by year, make, model and engine at the parts counter or on the box lookup.
  3. If your drain plug uses a crush washer, buy a new one. Reusing it is the top cause of drips.
Pro tip: Write the oil type, capacity and filter number on a card in the glovebox. Next time takes five minutes at the store.

Step 2Drain the old oil

  1. Drive onto ramps (or lift and set on jack stands), set the parking brake and chock the rear wheels.
  2. Open the hood and loosen the oil filler cap so the oil drains faster.
  3. Find the drain plug on the bottom of the oil pan, not the transmission. If unsure, check a model-specific photo before loosening anything.
  4. Put the pan slightly toward the direction the stream will shoot, loosen the plug, and remove the last turns by hand.
  5. Let it drain fully, about 5 to 10 minutes, then wipe the plug, fit the new washer and tighten it snug to the torque spec (often 20 to 30 ft-lb). Do not overtighten.
Caution: Overtightening a drain plug can strip the oil pan threads, which turns a $40 job into a $400 one.

Step 3Swap the filter

  1. Move the drain pan under the filter. Loosen it with the filter wrench and spin it off by hand; it will dribble.
  2. Make sure the old rubber gasket came off with the filter. A doubled gasket will leak badly.
  3. Rub a film of new oil on the new filter's gasket.
  4. Spin it on by hand until the gasket touches, then tighten about three-quarters of a turn more by hand, or per the filter's instructions.
  5. Cartridge-style filters (a paper element in a housing) also need the new O-ring from the kit. Tighten the cap to the spec on the housing.

Step 4Refill and check

  1. Lower the car to level ground. Pour in about a quart less than the full capacity.
  2. Start the engine for 30 seconds, watch for the oil light to go out, then shut it off and look under the car for drips.
  3. Wait two minutes, pull the dipstick, wipe it, reinsert and read. Top up a little at a time until it reaches the full mark.
  4. Reset the oil-life or service reminder (the procedure is in your owner's manual).
  5. Put the old oil in the empty bottles and drop the oil and filter at a parts store or recycling center.
Pro tip: Check the level again the next morning, cold and level. That's the most accurate reading.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Oil light stays on after startingShut off immediately. Check the oil level and that the filter is on correctly. Don't drive with the light on.
Drip from the drain plugOld washer reused or plug loose. Fit a new washer and tighten to spec.
Leak around the filterDouble gasket or not tight enough. Remove, check for the old gasket, reinstall.
Oil above the full markDrain a little out. Overfilling can foam the oil and damage seals.

When to stop and call a pro

  • The drain plug threads feel stripped or the plug won't tighten
  • You find a leak you can't trace, or oil on the exhaust
  • Your car needs the oil changed from the top with special tools (some European models)

Printable checklist

  • Right oil weight, spec and amount
  • New filter and drain washer
  • Car secure on ramps or stands
  • Drain plug tightened to spec
  • Old filter gasket removed
  • Refilled and level checked
  • No drips after running
  • Reminder reset
  • Old oil recycled
