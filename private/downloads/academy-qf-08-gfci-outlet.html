Replace a GFCI Outlet Safely

Swap a worn or dead GFCI outlet correctly, including the LINE and LOAD wiring most people get wrong.

Time: 30 to 60 minutesLevel: IntermediateSteps: 4

GFCI outlets protect you from shock in kitchens, bathrooms, garages and outdoors. They wear out over time and should trip when tested. Replacing one is a straightforward job if you shut the power off, verify it's off, and wire LINE and LOAD correctly.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Turn off the breaker and verify the power is off with a non-contact tester AND by testing the wires. Never trust the breaker label alone.
  • Never work on live wires.
  • Check your local permit rules. Some areas require a permit or licensed electrician for certain electrical work.
  • Stop and call a licensed electrician if you find aluminum wiring (dull silver wires), no ground and you're unsure, scorched or melted wires, or more wires than you can identify.
  • Use a GFCI with the same amp rating as the outlet you're replacing.
  • If you're unsure at any point, stop. Electrical mistakes can cause shock or fire.

Step 1Power off, verify off

  1. Plug a lamp or the plug-in tester into the outlet so you can see when power goes off.
  2. Turn off the breaker for that circuit. Put tape over the breaker with a note so no one turns it back on.
  3. Remove the cover plate and the outlet mounting screws, and gently pull the outlet out of the box.
  4. Test every wire with the non-contact tester. Then, if you have a multimeter, test between the black/hot and white/neutral, and hot to ground. Zero volts everywhere.
  5. Test your tester on a known live outlet first, so you know it works.

Step 2Label the wires

  1. One cable (one black, one white, one bare/green): it's all LINE. Easy.
  2. Two cables (two blacks, two whites): one brings power in (LINE), and one sends power to other outlets downstream (LOAD). You must know which is which.
  3. Look at how the old GFCI was wired: the terminals marked LINE and LOAD. If it was wired correctly and working, label the wires to match with tape before removing them.
  4. Not sure? Disconnect all wires, separate the cables so the bare ends can't touch anything or each other, put a wire nut on each. Turn the breaker on. Use the non-contact tester to find which black wire is hot. That cable is LINE. Turn the breaker off again and re-verify before continuing.
Caution: That last step involves a live circuit. Keep hands away from the wire ends, keep the ends capped and apart, and stand on a dry surface. If that makes you uncomfortable, call an electrician.

Step 3Wire the new GFCI

  1. Leave the tape over the LOAD terminals on the new GFCI unless you have a LOAD cable.
  2. Connect the LINE hot (black) to the LINE brass screw and the LINE neutral (white) to the LINE silver screw.
  3. If you have a LOAD cable, connect its black to the LOAD brass and white to the LOAD silver.
  4. Connect the bare or green ground wire to the green screw.
  5. Strip about 5/8" of insulation (use the strip gauge on the back of the outlet). For back-wire clamp terminals, insert straight and tighten the screw. For side screws, hook the wire clockwise around the screw and tighten firmly.
  6. Gently tug each wire to confirm it's tight. No bare copper should show beyond the terminal except the ground.

Step 4Mount, power up and test

  1. Fold the wires back into the box neatly and screw the outlet into the box, straight.
  2. Install the cover plate.
  3. Turn the breaker back on. Most new GFCIs come tripped. Press RESET.
  4. Plug in the GFCI tester and press its test button, or press the outlet's TEST button. Power should cut off. Press RESET to restore.
  5. If you wired LOAD, check that downstream outlets also lose power when the GFCI trips. Label those outlets 'GFCI Protected.'
  6. Test GFCIs monthly with the TEST button.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Aluminum wiring
  • Scorched, melted or brittle wires
  • No ground and you're not sure of code requirements
  • The breaker trips when you turn it on
  • Box too crowded to fit a GFCI safely
  • Anything you're unsure about

Printable job checklist

  • Breaker off and taped
  • Tester verified on a live outlet
  • All wires tested dead
  • LINE and LOAD labeled
  • Correct amp rating
  • LINE black to brass, white to silver
  • Ground to green
  • Wires tight, no extra bare copper
  • Breaker on, RESET, TEST, RESET
  • Downstream outlets labeled
