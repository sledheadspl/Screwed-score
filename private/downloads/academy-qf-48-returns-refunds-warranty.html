Returns, Refunds and Warranty Claims That Get Approved

Get refunds and warranty repairs approved, including when the store says no.

Time: 20 to 60 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Return policies are store rules; warranties are promises from the manufacturer or seller. Knowing which one applies, and documenting the defect, gets most claims approved. In the U.S., the federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act governs written consumer product warranties.

Read this first

  • Check return windows and warranty periods on day one and put them on your calendar.
  • Keep receipts and packaging until you're sure.
  • Don't make 'unauthorized repairs' before a warranty claim; it can be used to deny it.

What you need

  • Receipt or order number
  • Photos or video of the defect
  • Warranty terms

Step 1Pick the right path

  1. Inside the return window: return to the store.
  2. After it: manufacturer warranty claim.
  3. Check if your credit card offers extended warranty or purchase protection.

Step 2Document

  1. Video the defect happening.
  2. Write down when it started and what you've tried.

Step 3Ask the right way

  1. Say: 'This is defective and within warranty. I'd like a replacement or repair under the warranty.'
  2. If denied, ask for the reason in writing and a supervisor.
  3. Escalate to corporate customer service by email.

Step 4Last steps

  1. Card dispute if the item wasn't as described or never arrived.
  2. Complaint to the state attorney general or BBB; small claims for higher values.
