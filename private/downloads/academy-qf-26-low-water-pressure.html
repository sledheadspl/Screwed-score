Fix Low Water Pressure at a Faucet or Shower

Clear clogged aerators and showerheads, and check valves before calling a plumber.

Time: 15 to 45 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

If low pressure is at one fixture, it's almost always a clogged aerator, showerhead or cartridge. If it's the whole house, check the main valves and pressure regulator before assuming a big repair.

A plumber charges
$150 to $250
You'll spend
$0 to $20

Safety first, read before you start

  • Turn off the water to a fixture before taking it apart.
  • Plug the sink drain so small parts can't fall in.
  • Don't use excessive force on old valves; they can break.

What you'll learn

  • Tell a fixture problem from a whole-house problem
  • Clean aerators and showerheads
  • Check shutoff valves
  • Know when it's the regulator

What you need

  • Adjustable wrench or pliers
  • Rag (to protect finishes)
  • White vinegar and a bowl or bag
  • Old toothbrush
  • Pin

Step 1Narrow it down

  1. Test other faucets, hot and cold. One fixture low: fixture problem. Only hot low everywhere: water heater area. Whole house low: main supply.

Step 2Clean the aerator

  1. Unscrew the aerator at the faucet tip (wrap a rag on the jaws to protect the finish).
  2. Note the order of screens and washers.
  3. Soak in vinegar 30 minutes, scrub, and reassemble in order.

Step 3Clean the showerhead

  1. Unscrew the head, or tie a bag of vinegar around it overnight.
  2. Scrub the nozzles and poke clogged holes with a pin.
  3. Check for a flow restrictor screen inside the inlet and clean it too.

Step 4Whole-house checks

  1. Make sure the main shutoff valve is fully open.
  2. Check the shutoffs under the affected sink are fully open.
  3. If you have a pressure regulator (bell-shaped valve near the main), a failing one is a common cause; that's a plumber job.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Still low after cleaning the aeratorThe cartridge or valve inside the faucet is clogged.
Only hot side low at one faucetHot cartridge or supply clogged with sediment.
Pressure dropped suddenly house-wideCheck for a leak or call the water utility about work in the area.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Whole-house low pressure with valves open
  • Suspected pressure regulator failure
  • Corroded galvanized pipes

Printable checklist

  • Identified one-fixture vs whole-house
  • Aerator cleaned and reassembled in order
  • Showerhead soaked and restrictor cleaned
  • Shutoff valves fully open
  • Main valve fully open
