No Heat? The Checks Before You Call HVAC

Work through the free fixes, and change the filter right, before paying for a service call.

Time: 15 to 30 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

A surprising share of no-heat calls are thermostat settings, dead thermostat batteries, a switched-off furnace, a tripped breaker or a clogged filter. These checks take minutes and don't involve opening anything dangerous.

An HVAC service call costs
$100 to $200 before any repair
You'll spend
$0 to $30 (filter)

Safety first, read before you start

  • If you smell gas, leave the house now and call your gas company from outside. Don't touch switches.
  • Don't open the burner or electrical compartments or bypass any safety switch.
  • Install a carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas if you have any fuel-burning appliance.

What you'll learn

  • Run the thermostat checks
  • Check power and the furnace switch
  • Change the filter correctly
  • Recognize the furnace's error-code light

What you need

  • New filter of the exact size (printed on the old one's edge)
  • Fresh thermostat batteries if it uses them
  • Flashlight

Step 1Thermostat

  1. Set to HEAT, raise the setpoint 5 degrees above room temperature.
  2. Replace the batteries if the display is blank or weak.
  3. Check the schedule or 'hold' isn't overriding you.

Step 2Power

  1. Find the furnace's service switch (looks like a light switch on or near the unit) and make sure it's on.
  2. Check the breaker labeled furnace or HVAC.
  3. Make sure the furnace door panel is fully closed; a safety switch keeps it off if not.

Step 3Filter

  1. Turn the system off. Slide out the filter at the furnace or the return grille.
  2. If it's gray and clogged, that alone can cause overheating shutdowns.
  3. Fit the new one with the airflow arrow pointing toward the furnace.
  4. Note the date. Many 1-inch filters need changing every 1 to 3 months.
Pro tip: Very high-MERV filters can restrict airflow in some systems. Use the rating your furnace manual recommends.

Step 4Read the status light

  1. Many furnaces have a small window with a blinking LED. Count the blinks.
  2. Match the code to the chart printed inside the panel door. It tells you or the tech exactly what failed (for example a flame sensor or pressure switch).

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Blower runs, no heatCould be ignition or flame sensor. Note the blink code and call a pro.
Heats then shuts off quicklyOften a clogged filter or blocked vents. Replace filter and open all supply vents.
Nothing at allThermostat, switch, breaker, door panel. Then call.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Any gas smell
  • Repeated lockout codes
  • Burner, ignition or flame sensor issues
  • Cracked heat exchanger concerns or CO alarm going off

Printable checklist

  • Thermostat on HEAT and set higher
  • Batteries replaced
  • Service switch on
  • Breaker on
  • Door panel closed
  • Filter replaced, arrow toward furnace
  • Blink code noted
