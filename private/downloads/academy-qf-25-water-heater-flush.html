Flush a Water Heater and Test the Relief Valve

Drain sediment and check the safety valve on a standard tank heater, safely.

Time: 1 to 2 hoursLevel: IntermediateSteps: 4

Sediment settles in the bottom of a tank, making it noisy, inefficient and short-lived. A yearly flush helps, and testing the temperature-and-pressure (T&P) relief valve confirms the tank's most important safety device works.

A plumber charges
$100 to $200
You'll spend
$0 to $15 (hose)

Safety first, read before you start

  • Water in the tank can be scalding. Let it cool for a few hours or wear gloves and direct water safely.
  • Electric heater: turn off the breaker before draining. Never power the elements with the tank empty.
  • Gas heater: set the control to 'pilot' or 'vacation' before draining; follow the label on the heater.
  • If the relief valve won't close after testing, or keeps dripping, call a plumber. Don't plug or cap it.

What you'll learn

  • Shut down gas or electric heaters safely
  • Drain and flush sediment
  • Test the T&P valve
  • Refill and restart correctly

What you need

  • Garden hose
  • Bucket
  • Flathead screwdriver (some drain valves)
  • Gloves

Step 1Shut it down

  1. Electric: breaker off. Gas: control knob to pilot or vacation.
  2. Close the cold-water supply valve on top of the tank.
  3. Open a hot tap in the house to let air in.

Step 2Drain and flush

  1. Attach a hose to the drain valve at the bottom and run it to a floor drain or outside, lower than the valve.
  2. Open the drain valve and let it run until the water runs clear.
  3. Briefly open the cold supply with the drain open to stir up remaining sediment, then close it again; repeat until clear.
  4. Close the drain valve and remove the hose.

Step 3Test the relief valve

  1. With the tank refilled and pressurized, put a bucket under the discharge pipe.
  2. Lift the valve lever briefly. Water should rush out, then stop when you release it.
Caution: If water keeps flowing or it won't seat, call a plumber. A faulty relief valve is a safety problem.

Step 4Refill and restart

  1. Open the cold supply and leave the hot tap open until water flows without air sputtering.
  2. Electric: only now turn the breaker on. Gas: return the control to its setting.
  3. Check the drain valve for drips.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Drain flows weaklySediment clogged it. Open and close it a few times with the cold supply on briefly.
Drain valve drips after closingCap the hose thread or replace the valve.
No hot water after refill (electric)Breaker turned on with an empty tank may have burned out an element.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Relief valve leaks or won't reseat
  • Water pooling under the tank (tank leak)
  • Rust-colored water from the hot side only
  • Gas control or pilot problems

Printable checklist

  • Power or gas set to off/pilot
  • Cold supply closed
  • Hot tap open
  • Drained until clear
  • Drain closed
  • Relief valve tested
  • Tank full before power on
  • No leaks
