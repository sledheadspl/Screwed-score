Audit a Medical Bill Line by Line

Get the itemized bill, spot duplicate and inflated charges, and dispute them in writing.

Time: 1 to 2 hoursLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

The summary bill you get first hides the details. The itemized bill shows every code and charge, and that's where duplicate charges, services you didn't receive and coding errors show up. You have the right to ask for it, and asking often starts a review on its own.

Read this first

  • Don't ignore a medical bill while you dispute it. Call, say you're disputing, and ask them to put the account on hold in writing.
  • Keep every letter and note every call: date, time, name, what was said.
  • Never pay a bill you're disputing in full until the review is done, unless it's going to collections; then pay under written protest or negotiate.

What you'll learn

  • Request the itemized bill and your records
  • Read CPT codes and line items
  • Spot the most common errors
  • Dispute in writing and track it

What you need

  • The bill and your insurance Explanation of Benefits (EOB) for the same date
  • Your own notes of the visit (times, tests you remember)
  • A notebook or spreadsheet

Step 1Get the right documents

  1. Call billing and ask for a fully itemized bill with CPT/HCPCS codes, not a summary.
  2. Ask for the matching insurance EOB from your insurer (online portal or by phone).
  3. If something looks wrong, request your medical records for that visit to compare against.

Step 2Spot the errors

  1. Duplicate lines: the same code on the same day more than once.
  2. Services you didn't get: tests, medications or supplies you don't remember.
  3. Wrong quantity or wrong time (for example, operating room time longer than the procedure).
  4. Upcoding: a more expensive code than the visit supports (for example a level-5 office visit for a short check).
  5. Charges your EOB shows as denied or 'not billable to patient'.
Pro tip: Search the CPT code number online to see what it actually describes. If the description doesn't match what happened, flag it.

Step 3Dispute it

  1. Write a short letter or portal message listing each disputed line, the code, the amount and why you're disputing it.
  2. Ask for corrected charges and a written response, and ask them to hold the account from collections during review.
  3. If your insurer processed it wrong, appeal with the insurer too (see the EOB lesson).
  4. Follow up every 2 weeks and keep notes.

Step 4Then negotiate what's left

  1. Ask for the self-pay or prompt-pay discount.
  2. Ask about financial assistance (see the hospital assistance lesson).
  3. Ask for an interest-free payment plan in writing if you can't pay in full.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
They won't send an itemized billAsk in writing, and mention you can't pay a bill you can't verify. Escalate to the patient advocate or billing supervisor.
Sent to collections during the disputeSend the collector a written dispute (see the debt collector lesson) and tell the provider in writing.

Get extra help if

  • The bill is very large and the provider won't engage
  • You suspect fraud (services never provided); report to your insurer's fraud line
  • A nonprofit patient advocate or your state insurance department can help for free

Printable checklist

  • Itemized bill with codes
  • Matching EOB
  • Duplicates checked
  • Services I didn't get flagged
  • Codes looked up
  • Written dispute sent
  • Hold requested
  • Calls logged
