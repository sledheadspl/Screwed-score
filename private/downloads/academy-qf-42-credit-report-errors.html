Fix Errors on Your Credit Report

Get your free reports, find errors and dispute them so they're corrected or removed.

Time: 1 to 2 hoursLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, credit bureaus must investigate disputes, usually within 30 days. Anything a credit repair company can legally do, you can do yourself for free.

Credit repair companies charge
$50 to $150 a month
You'll spend
$0

Read this first

  • Get reports only from AnnualCreditReport.com, the official free source.
  • Never pay anyone who promises to remove accurate negative information; that's a common scam.
  • Dispute each error with every bureau that shows it.

What you'll learn

  • Pull all three reports free
  • Spot common errors
  • Dispute with the bureaus and the furnisher
  • Follow through

What you need

  • ID details for the reports
  • Statements or letters proving the error

Step 1Get the reports

  1. Go to AnnualCreditReport.com and request Equifax, Experian and TransUnion reports.
  2. Download and save each as a PDF.

Step 2Find errors

  1. Accounts that aren't yours (possible identity theft).
  2. Wrong late payments, wrong balances or limits.
  3. Paid debts still showing as unpaid.
  4. Duplicate accounts or old negatives past the reporting period (most negatives fall off after 7 years).

Step 3Dispute

  1. File online with each bureau showing the error, or by mail with copies of your proof.
  2. Also dispute directly with the company that reported it (the furnisher).
  3. Keep confirmation numbers and copies.
Pro tip: Be specific: 'Account ending 1234 shows a 30-day late in March 2025. I paid on time; statement attached.'

Step 4Follow through

  1. Bureaus generally have 30 days to investigate (45 if you send more information during the dispute).
  2. Check the results letter. If not fixed, add a brief statement or re-dispute with new evidence.
  3. If it's identity theft, also report at IdentityTheft.gov and consider a credit freeze.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Bureau says 'verified'Ask for how it was verified, dispute with the furnisher directly, and file a CFPB complaint if wrong.
Same error returnsFile a CFPB complaint and keep records.

Get extra help if

  • Identity theft across many accounts
  • Repeated failure to correct: a consumer attorney may help (some FCRA cases allow fee recovery)

Printable checklist

  • 3 reports downloaded
  • Errors listed
  • Each bureau disputed
  • Furnisher disputed
  • Proof attached
  • Results checked
  • Freeze if identity theft
