Wiper Blades and Washer Fixes

Replace blades the right size, and fix washers that dribble, freeze or won't spray.

Time: 10 to 30 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 3

Worn wipers are a safety problem in the first heavy rain. Blades take five minutes. Washer problems are usually a clog, a pump or a fuse, all cheap to find.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Turn the car off before working on the wipers. A wiper arm switching on can crush fingers.
  • Never let a bare wiper arm snap back onto the glass. It can crack the windshield. Lay a towel on the glass.
  • Use washer fluid, not dish soap or plain water in freezing weather.

What you'll learn

  • Find your exact blade sizes
  • Swap blades on the common hook and pin arms
  • Clear clogged washer nozzles
  • Tell a dead pump from a blown fuse

What you need

  • Correct blades (driver, passenger and rear if fitted)
  • Towel
  • Safety pin or thin needle
  • Washer fluid rated for your winter temperatures

Step 1Get the right blades

  1. Look up the sizes in the parts store's book or website by year, make and model. Driver and passenger are usually different lengths.
  2. Beam-style blades handle snow better; conventional frame blades are cheaper. Either is fine for most climates.

Step 2Swap the blades

  1. Lift the arm off the glass and lay a towel on the windshield under it.
  2. Find the release tab where the blade meets the arm. Press it and slide the blade down and off the hook.
  3. Slide the new blade's adapter onto the hook until it clicks. Tug it to make sure it's locked.
  4. Lower the arm gently. Repeat on the other side and the rear.
Pro tip: Many cars have a 'service position' that parks the wipers higher so you can lift them under the hood edge. It's in the owner's manual.

Step 3Fix washers that won't spray

  1. Check the reservoir isn't empty or frozen.
  2. Pull the washer lever and listen near the reservoir. A hum means the pump runs and the problem is a clog or kinked hose.
  3. No hum: check the washer fuse (see the fuse box diagram). If the fuse is good and still no hum, the pump is likely bad.
  4. For a clogged nozzle, gently push a pin into the nozzle hole and wiggle, then spray. Aim the jet back to the middle of the glass with the pin.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Streaks with new bladesClean the glass with glass cleaner and wipe each blade edge with a damp cloth.
Blade chattersArm may be twisted; ask a shop to check the arm angle.
Pump hums but no sprayHose split or off at a nozzle. Follow the hose from the reservoir.

When to stop and call a pro

  • The wiper motor or linkage is broken (arms don't move at all with a good fuse)
  • The pump needs bumper or wheel-liner removal you're not comfortable with

Printable checklist

  • Right sizes for each blade
  • Towel on glass
  • Blades clicked and tug-tested
  • Washer fluid topped up
  • Nozzles clear and aimed
  • Fuse checked if no spray
