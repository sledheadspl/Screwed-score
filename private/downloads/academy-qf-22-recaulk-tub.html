Recaulk a Tub or Shower So It Stays Clean

Remove moldy caulk and lay a clean, watertight bead that actually lasts.

Time: 1 to 2 hours, plus 24 hours to cureLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Failed caulk lets water behind tile and into walls and floors, which is where the expensive damage happens. A new bead is cheap; the secret is removing every bit of the old caulk and letting the joint dry completely.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Ventilate the room; some removers and sealants have strong fumes.
  • Use plastic razor blades on tubs and fiberglass to avoid scratches.
  • If you find soft drywall, rotten wood or mold beyond the joint, stop. That's water damage that needs repair first.

What you'll learn

  • Remove old caulk completely
  • Kill mold in the joint
  • Lay a straight, smooth bead
  • Let it cure properly

What you need

  • 100% silicone kitchen and bath caulk (mold-resistant)
  • Caulk gun
  • Plastic razor scraper and utility knife
  • Rubbing alcohol and rags
  • Painter's tape
  • Diluted bleach or mold cleaner

Step 1Remove the old caulk

  1. Slice along both edges of the old bead with a utility knife.
  2. Peel and scrape it out completely. Silicone won't stick to old silicone.
  3. Clean the joint with mold cleaner, rinse, then wipe with rubbing alcohol to remove soap film and residue.

Step 2Dry completely

  1. Let the joint dry at least 24 hours (run a fan). Moisture trapped under new caulk is what breeds the mold line.
  2. Fill the tub with water before caulking. It flexes the tub to its loaded position, so the bead doesn't crack later.

Step 3Lay the bead

  1. Put painter's tape on both sides of the joint, leaving a gap about the width of the bead you want.
  2. Cut the nozzle small, at an angle. You can always cut more.
  3. Push the bead along the joint in one steady motion.
  4. Smooth it with a gloved finger or tool dipped in soapy water, then pull the tape off right away, at an angle away from the bead.
Pro tip: Do one wall at a time so the caulk doesn't skin over before you smooth it.

Step 4Cure

  1. Leave the water in the tub and keep the shower off for the time on the tube (often 24 hours).
  2. Drain the tub after curing.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Caulk won't stickOld silicone residue or moisture. Remove, clean with alcohol, dry, redo.
Bead lumpySmooth faster, or remove while wet and redo a short section.
Mold returns quicklyJoint wasn't dry or ventilation is poor. Run the fan 20 minutes after showers.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Soft walls, loose tiles or rot behind the joint
  • Leaks below the tub (ceiling stains downstairs)
  • Large gaps that need backer rod or grout repair

Printable checklist

  • Old caulk fully removed
  • Cleaned and alcohol-wiped
  • Dried 24 hours
  • Tub filled with water
  • Taped both sides
  • Smoothed and tape pulled wet
  • Cured before use
