Unclog Any Drain Without Chemicals

Clear sinks, tubs and toilets with simple tools, and keep them from clogging again.

Time: 15 to 60 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 5

Chemical drain cleaners often don't fix a clog and can damage pipes and burn skin. A plunger, a cheap plastic barbed tool and a hand auger clear most household clogs. This guide shows which tool to use where.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Never mix drain chemicals, and never plunge or auger a drain where a chemical cleaner was just used. It can splash back. Flush with water first and wear eye protection.
  • Wear gloves and safety glasses.
  • Keep a bucket and towels ready when opening any trap.
  • If several drains are backing up at once, or sewage comes up in a tub or floor drain, it's likely the main line. Stop using water and call a pro.

Tools

  • Cup plunger (flat, for sinks and tubs)
  • Flange plunger (with a fold-out cup, for toilets)
  • Plastic barbed drain tool
  • 1/4" hand drum auger (about 25 ft)
  • Toilet (closet) auger (optional)
  • Bucket, towels, gloves, glasses
  • Channel-lock pliers
  • Wet/dry vacuum (optional)

Step 1Which drain is it?

  1. Just one fixture slow or clogged: the clog is close to that fixture. You can fix this.
  2. Multiple drains slow, gurgling, or water coming up in a tub when the toilet flushes: likely a main line clog. Call a pro.
  3. Bathroom sinks and tubs: usually hair and soap. Kitchen: grease and food. Toilets: too much paper or something that shouldn't be flushed.

Step 2Bathroom sink and tub

  1. Remove the stopper. On a pop-up sink stopper, the pivot nut on the back of the drain pipe under the sink releases it.
  2. Push the plastic barbed tool down the drain, twist, and pull out slowly. Expect a gross clump of hair. Repeat until it comes out clean.
  3. Run hot tap water for a minute to test.
  4. For a tub, remove the overflow plate (2 screws) so you can plunge with it covered by a wet rag, or feed the auger through the overflow.
Pro tip: Most bathroom clogs are within a few inches of the stopper. The barbed tool fixes a lot of them in under 5 minutes.

Step 3Kitchen sink

  1. If you have a garbage disposal, turn it off at the switch (and unplug it if possible). Press the reset button on the bottom of the unit and try once.
  2. On a double sink, plug the other side tightly with a stopper or wet rag. Otherwise plunging just pushes water across.
  3. Fill the sink with a few inches of water, seal the cup plunger over the drain, and plunge firmly 10 to 15 times.
  4. Still clogged? Put a bucket under the P-trap (the U-shaped pipe), loosen the slip nuts by hand or with pliers, and clean it out.
  5. With the trap off, feed the hand auger into the pipe going into the wall, crank, push and pull to break the clog.
  6. Reinstall the trap with the washers seated, hand-tight plus a little more, and run water to check for leaks.

Step 4Toilet

  1. Don't keep flushing. If the bowl is high, wait for it to drain down, or bail some water into a bucket.
  2. Use a flange plunger with the flange pushed into the drain hole. Make sure the cup is covered with water.
  3. Start gently to push out air, then do 15 to 20 firm push-pull strokes, keeping the seal.
  4. Still clogged? Use a toilet auger: insert it with the bend in the bowl's drain, crank as you feed the cable in, then pull back while cranking.
  5. Flush once to test, with your hand ready on the shutoff valve.

Step 5Keep it from coming back

  1. Hair catchers in every sink and tub.
  2. Wipe grease into the trash before washing pans. Never pour grease down the drain.
  3. Run hot water for 30 seconds after kitchen use.
  4. Only toilet paper goes in toilets. 'Flushable' wipes can still cause clogs.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Multiple drains backed up, or sewage in a tub or floor drain
  • Clog returns within days after clearing
  • Recurring clogs that may be roots in the sewer line
  • Old metal drain pipes that crack or leak when touched
