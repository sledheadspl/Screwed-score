Tire Pressure, Tread, Rotation and the TPMS Light

Read your tires like a tech so nobody sells you a set you don't need yet.

Time: 20 to 45 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Correct pressure and regular rotation decide how long tires last and how the car stops. Tread depth tells you, in numbers, whether you need new tires, which is the best defense against a hard sell.

A shop charges
$30 to $80, plus unneeded tire sales
You'll spend
$10 to $30 (gauge and tread tool)

Safety first, read before you start

  • Set pressures from the sticker in the driver's door jamb, not the number on the tire sidewall (that's the maximum).
  • Never get under a car on a jack alone. Use jack stands if rotating.
  • Tighten lug nuts in a star pattern to the torque spec, and recheck after 50 miles.

What you'll learn

  • Set the right pressure, cold
  • Measure tread depth and read wear patterns
  • Rotate tires in the right pattern
  • Reset or troubleshoot the TPMS light
  • Know the legal and safe replacement points

What you need

  • Digital tire pressure gauge
  • Tread depth gauge (or a penny/quarter)
  • Air source
  • For rotation: jack, two jack stands, lug wrench, torque wrench

Step 1Set pressure the right way

  1. Check cold, before driving or after the car has sat 3 hours.
  2. Read the target from the door-jamb sticker. Front and rear can differ.
  3. Check all four (and the spare, if it has air). Add or let out air until each matches.
  4. Pressure drops about 1 psi for every 10 °F the temperature falls, so recheck when seasons change.

Step 2Measure tread and read wear

  1. Measure in the main grooves at the inside, center and outside of each tire.
  2. In the U.S., 2/32 inch is the legal minimum in most states; many experts recommend replacing at 4/32 for wet roads.
  3. Worn in the center: overinflated. Worn on both edges: underinflated. One edge: alignment.
  4. Look for bulges, cords showing, or cracks. Any of those means replace now, regardless of tread.
Pro tip: Write down the numbers. If a shop says you need tires, compare their measurement to yours.

Step 3Rotate the tires

  1. Check your owner's manual for the pattern and interval (often every 5,000 to 7,500 miles).
  2. Loosen lug nuts slightly on the ground, then lift and support on jack stands.
  3. Move tires in the pattern (a common one for front-wheel drive: fronts straight back, rears cross to the front).
  4. Snug the lugs in a star pattern, lower the car, then torque to spec.
Caution: Directional tires (arrow on the sidewall) must stay on the same side. Staggered sizes can't be rotated front to back.

Step 4Handle the TPMS light

  1. Solid light: at least one tire is low. Check and set all four, then drive 10 to 15 minutes; it often clears on its own.
  2. Some cars need a reset button or menu procedure after setting pressures (owner's manual).
  3. Flashing for about a minute then solid: a sensor or system fault, not just low air. A shop with a TPMS tool can diagnose it.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
One tire keeps losing airSpray soapy water on the tread and valve stem to find the bubbles; a nail can often be plugged-and-patched cheaply.
Car pulls after rotationA tire may have a belt issue or the alignment is off. Swap fronts left-right to test.
TPMS light after rotationSome systems need a relearn after rotation. Check the manual.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Sidewall damage, bulges or cords showing
  • Repeated air loss you can't find
  • Uneven wear that suggests alignment or suspension problems
  • TPMS fault (flashing light)

Printable checklist

  • Pressures set cold to door sticker
  • Spare checked
  • Tread measured at 3 points, written down
  • Wear pattern checked
  • Rotation pattern per manual
  • Lugs torqued and rechecked after 50 miles
  • TPMS reset if required
