Fix a Running Toilet for Good

Find out exactly why it keeps running and fix it in 30 minutes with a $10 to $25 part.

Time: 15 to 45 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

A running toilet can waste a lot of water every day and quietly push up your water bill. Nearly every case comes down to three parts: the flapper, the fill valve, or the chain and float adjustment. This guide shows you which one and how to fix it.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Turn off the water at the shutoff valve behind the toilet before replacing parts.
  • The tank lid is heavy porcelain. Set it on a towel on the floor where it won't get stepped on or knocked over.
  • Wear gloves; tank water is clean but parts can be grimy.
  • Don't overtighten plastic nuts or tank bolts. Porcelain cracks.

Step 1Find the cause

  1. Take the tank lid off and watch a full flush and refill.
  2. Water spilling into the overflow tube (the open pipe in the middle): the water level is set too high, or the fill valve isn't shutting off.
  3. Hissing or the tank refilling every so often by itself: water is leaking from the tank into the bowl. Do the dye test.
  4. Dye test: put 5 to 10 drops of food coloring in the tank. Don't flush. Wait 15 to 30 minutes. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.
  5. Handle has to be jiggled: the chain is too long and catching, or too short and holding the flapper open.

Step 2Quick fixes first (free)

  1. Chain: with the flapper closed, the chain should have about 1/2" of slack. Adjust which link hooks on the handle arm and trim extra so it can't get trapped under the flapper.
  2. Water level: it should sit about 1" below the top of the overflow tube, or at the fill line marked inside the tank.
  3. On a float-cup fill valve (cup slides on the valve shaft), squeeze the clip or turn the adjustment screw to lower the cup.
  4. On a ball float (ball on an arm), turn the screw at the top of the valve, or gently bend the arm down to lower the level.
  5. Clean the flush valve seat (the ring the flapper seals against) with a sponge or soft scrub pad. Mineral buildup there causes slow leaks.

Step 3Replace the flapper

  1. Turn off the water at the shutoff valve and flush to drain the tank.
  2. Unhook the chain from the handle arm. Slide the flapper's ears off the pegs on the overflow tube (or remove the ring if it has one).
  3. Take the old flapper to the store to match the size and style.
  4. Install the new one, center it over the drain, attach the chain with about 1/2" of slack.
  5. Turn the water on, let the tank fill, and redo the dye test.
Pro tip: If you have an adjustable flapper, setting it to close sooner saves water per flush. Just make sure the toilet still flushes cleanly.

Step 4Replace the fill valve

  1. Water off. Flush, then sponge out the remaining water in the tank. Put a towel under the supply line.
  2. Disconnect the supply line from the bottom of the tank.
  3. Unscrew the large plastic locknut under the tank holding the old fill valve, and lift the valve out.
  4. Adjust the new valve's height per its instructions (the critical mark is usually at least 1" above the overflow tube).
  5. Install it with its washer inside the tank, tighten the locknut hand-tight plus about a quarter turn.
  6. Clip the refill tube to the overflow tube so it aims into the tube. Don't push it down below the water line.
  7. Reconnect the supply line, turn the water on slowly, and check for drips. Set the water level.
Caution: If the shutoff valve won't turn or leaks when you move it, stop. Shut the main water off to the house and call a plumber or replace that valve.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Water leaking from the base of the toilet onto the floor (wax ring)
  • A cracked tank or bowl
  • Shutoff valve that won't close or leaks
  • Leaks between tank and bowl that need the tank removed, if you're not comfortable
