Store and Protect Your Collection

Sleeves, toploaders, binders, humidity and light, done right.

Time: 30 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Most damage comes from handling, bending, moisture and sunlight. Simple, inexpensive storage keeps cards in the condition you bought them.

Damaged cards can lose
Most of their value
You'll spend
$10 to $50

Read this first

  • Avoid rubber bands, paper clips and tight stacks.
  • Avoid PVC pages; choose acid-free, PVC-free products.
  • Keep away from direct sunlight, heat and damp basements.

What you'll learn

  • Sleeve and toploader correctly
  • Choose binders that don't damage cards
  • Control humidity and light
  • Organize and insure

What you need

  • Penny sleeves
  • Toploaders or semi-rigid holders
  • Side-loading, PVC-free binder
  • Storage boxes
  • Silica gel packs (optional)

Step 1Sleeve right

  1. Slide cards into penny sleeves top-first, gently.
  2. Valuable cards go into a toploader too.

Step 2Binders

  1. Use side-loading, PVC-free pages; avoid ring binders that dent cards near the rings.
  2. Don't overfill pockets.

Step 3Environment

  1. Store in a cool, dry room, not an attic, garage or basement.
  2. Keep out of direct sunlight; it fades cards.
  3. Use silica gel in sealed boxes in humid climates.

Step 4Organize and insure

  1. Keep a list or spreadsheet with values.
  2. Photograph high-value cards.
  3. Ask your insurer whether collectibles are covered or need a rider.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Cards curlingHumidity changes; store flat in a stable room.
Dents from binder ringsSwitch to a side-loading zip binder.
