Fix a Leaky Faucet (Any Type)

Identify your faucet type, get the right part, and stop the drip for good.

Time: 30 to 60 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

A dripping faucet wastes water and money, and it usually comes down to one small worn part. The trick is knowing which of the four faucet types you have, because each has a different fix. This guide walks you through identifying it and replacing the part.

Safety first, read before you start

  • Turn off both hot and cold shutoff valves under the sink, then open the faucet to drain pressure.
  • Plug the drain with a stopper or rag so small parts can't fall in.
  • Lay parts out on a towel in the order you remove them and take photos.
  • Use rags on wrenches over chrome to avoid scratches.
  • If the shutoff valves don't work, shut the main water off to the house.

Step 1Identify your faucet type

  1. Compression: two handles that you tighten down to stop water, and they feel softer as they close. Uses rubber washers. The most common cause of drips in older faucets.
  2. Cartridge: one or two handles, smooth motion. A single handle lifts up and down and swivels. Uses a replaceable cartridge.
  3. Ball: single handle on a rounded cap over a ball, common on older Delta faucets.
  4. Ceramic disc: single lever over a wide cylinder body. Very durable. Leaks are usually the seals.
  5. Look for a brand name on the faucet. If you can find the model, you can order exact parts.

Step 2Take it apart

  1. Water off, faucet open to drain, drain plugged.
  2. Pop off the decorative cap on the handle (a thin flathead works) to find the handle screw. Some handles use a small set screw on the side or back with an Allen key.
  3. Remove the handle.
  4. Compression: unscrew the packing nut and stem with a wrench.
  5. Cartridge: remove the retaining clip or nut and pull the cartridge straight up. Note its orientation for reinstall.
  6. Ball: unscrew the cap, lift out the cam and ball, then the seats and springs with needle-nose pliers.
  7. Ceramic disc: remove the escutcheon cap and unscrew the disc cylinder.
Pro tip: Take the old part to the hardware store. Matching by eye is much more reliable than guessing from the brand alone.

Step 3Replace the worn part

  1. Compression: replace the rubber washer at the bottom of the stem (held by a brass screw) and the O-ring on the stem. If the seat inside the faucet is pitted, replace it with a seat wrench.
  2. Cartridge: install a new cartridge in the same orientation. Hot/cold reversed after install means it's rotated 180 degrees.
  3. Ball: install new seats and springs (springs go in first, seats over them, cupped side out), and new O-rings on the body if it leaks at the spout.
  4. Ceramic disc: replace the neoprene seals on the bottom of the cylinder. Clean mineral buildup with vinegar.
  5. Coat O-rings and rubber parts with a thin layer of silicone plumber's grease.

Step 4Reassemble and test

  1. Reassemble in reverse order using your photos.
  2. Tighten snug, not hard. Overtightening damages new washers and cracks plastic parts.
  3. Open the faucet partway, then turn the shutoff valves on slowly.
  4. Let water run for a minute to clear air and debris, then shut it off and watch for drips for 5 minutes.
  5. Check under the sink for any leaks at the valves.

When to stop and call a pro

  • Shutoff valves leak or won't turn
  • Leaks inside the cabinet from the faucet body or supply lines that won't stop
  • Corroded parts that won't come apart without breaking
  • Faucet so old that parts aren't available (replacement may be smarter)
