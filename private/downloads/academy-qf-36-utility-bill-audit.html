Audit Your Utility Bill for Errors

Read your meter, check your rate plan and get billing errors corrected.

Time: 30 to 60 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

Electric, gas and water bills are often based on estimated readings, and customers are sometimes on a rate plan that doesn't fit their use. Comparing your meter to the bill and checking your rate is free and quick.

Read this first

  • Don't open meter enclosures or tamper with seals. Only read the visible display.
  • Keep paying undisputed amounts while you dispute the rest to avoid shutoff notices.
  • If you can't pay, ask about payment plans and assistance before a shutoff date.

Step 1Check the reading

  1. Find 'actual' or 'estimated' (often an 'E' next to the reading) on the bill.
  2. Photograph your meter display with the date.
  3. Compare to the bill's current reading. A big gap means an estimate was off.

Step 2Check usage patterns

  1. Compare the same month last year. A sudden jump with no change in your life suggests an error or a leak (for water, check for a running toilet).
  2. Check the number of days in the billing period; longer periods mean bigger bills.

Step 3Check the rate plan

  1. Look for your rate code on the bill and the utility's rate options online.
  2. Time-of-use plans help if you can shift usage to off-peak; they hurt if you can't.
  3. Ask about budget billing, low-income programs and seasonal discounts.

Step 4Dispute

  1. Call with your meter photo and ask for an actual read and a corrected bill.
  2. If unresolved, file a complaint with your state public utility commission; they handle billing disputes for regulated utilities.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Water usage high every monthDo a leak test: turn off all water and watch the meter for 30 minutes.
Utility won't correct itFile with the state utility commission.

Get extra help if

  • You face a shutoff notice: ask about protections and assistance programs right away
  • The utility won't respond: your state public utility commission
