Read Your Insurance EOB and Appeal a Denial

Understand what your insurer paid, why, and how to appeal a denial step by step.

Time: 1 to 3 hoursLevel: BeginnerSteps: 4

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is not a bill. It shows what the provider billed, what the insurer allowed, what it paid, and what it says you owe. When a claim is denied, you have the right to an internal appeal and, for many plans, an independent external review.

Read this first

  • Appeal deadlines are strict. Under many plans you have 180 days from the denial notice for an internal appeal; check your notice for your plan's deadline.
  • Keep copies of everything you send and use a method with proof of delivery.
  • For urgent care situations, ask for an expedited appeal.

What you'll learn

  • Read every column of an EOB
  • Find the denial reason code

  • Request external review if needed

What you need

  • The EOB and denial letter
  • Your plan's benefits booklet (Summary of Benefits and Coverage)
  • A letter of medical necessity from your doctor, if relevant

Step 1Read the EOB

  1. Billed amount: what the provider charged.
  2. Allowed amount: the negotiated price for in-network care. You shouldn't owe more than this for in-network care, except in limited cases.
  3. Plan paid, then your share: deductible, copay, coinsurance.
  4. Reason or remark codes explain adjustments and denials; the key to them is on the EOB or the insurer's site.

Step 2Fix simple errors first

  1. Call the insurer: was it a coding or missing-information denial? Many are fixed when the provider resubmits.
  2. Ask the provider's billing office to correct and resubmit if the error is theirs.

Step 3File the internal appeal

  1. Write a letter with your name, member ID, claim number and date of service.
  2. Quote the denial reason, and explain why it's wrong, citing your plan booklet's coverage language.
  3. Attach the doctor's letter of medical necessity and relevant records.
  4. Ask for a written decision, and note the date you sent it.
Pro tip: Ask the insurer for the specific clinical criteria they used. You're generally entitled to them, and they tell you exactly what evidence to send.

Step 4External review

  1. If the internal appeal is denied, the letter should explain how to request an independent external review.
  2. Request it within the deadline stated (often 4 months for many plans).
  3. Your state insurance department can help, especially for state-regulated plans.

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
No response by the deadlineCall, note the date, and contact your state insurance department.
Denied as 'out of network' for an emergencySee the No Surprises Act lesson; emergency care has special protections.

Get extra help if

  • High-cost treatment is denied
  • You're running out of time on an urgent appeal
  • Your state's consumer assistance program or a patient advocate can help for free

Printable checklist

  • EOB columns understood
  • Reason code looked up
  • Simple errors called in
  • Appeal letter with claim number
  • Doctor's letter attached
  • Sent with proof of delivery
  • Deadline on calendar
  • External review requested if denied
