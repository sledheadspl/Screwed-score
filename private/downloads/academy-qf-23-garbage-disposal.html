Unjam and Reset a Garbage Disposal

Get a humming or silent disposal working again without a plumber.

Time: 10 to 30 minutesLevel: BeginnerSteps: 3

Most 'broken' disposals are jammed or tripped. A humming disposal is jammed; a silent one has tripped its overload button or lost power. Both are usually fixed in minutes from under the sink.

A plumber charges
$150 to $250
You'll spend
$0 to $10

Safety first, read before you start

  • Never put your hand in the disposal. Use tongs or pliers.
  • Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit (or switch off its breaker) before working on it.
  • Don't use chemical drain cleaners in a disposal; they damage it and can splash back.

What you'll learn

  • Tell jammed from tripped
  • Free a jam with a hex key
  • Reset the overload
  • Prevent jams

What you need

  • 1/4" hex (Allen) key, often the one that came with the unit
  • Flashlight
  • Tongs or pliers
  • Bucket

Step 1Diagnose

  1. Flip the switch. Humming but not spinning: jammed. Nothing at all: tripped reset button, breaker or power.
  2. Turn the switch off and unplug the unit or turn off its breaker before continuing.

Step 2Free a jam

  1. Find the hex socket in the center of the bottom of the unit.
  2. Insert the hex key and work it back and forth until it turns a full circle freely.
  3. From above, with a flashlight, remove the object that caused the jam with tongs or pliers.

Step 3Reset and test

  1. Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit. If it doesn't stay in, wait 10 minutes for it to cool.
  2. Restore power, run cold water, and switch on.
  3. If still silent, check the breaker and the outlet (some are on a GFCI outlet that has tripped).

Troubleshooting

If this happensDo this
Resets but trips againStill jammed or overloaded. Free it again and feed food slowly with plenty of cold water.
Leaks from the bottomInternal seal failure; the unit usually needs replacing.
Leaks at the sink flange or drain pipeTighten the mounting ring or drain connection; replace the gasket if needed.

When to stop and call a pro

  • The motor hums with no jam
  • Leaks from the unit's body
  • Electrical smell or scorching at the outlet

Printable checklist

  • Switch off and unplugged
  • Hand never in the unit
  • Hex key turns freely
  • Object removed
  • Reset button pressed
  • Breaker or GFCI checked
  • Run with cold water
