# How to Find and Cancel Subscriptions You Forgot About

Subscriptions are designed to be easy to start and easy to forget. A free trial turns into a monthly charge, two family members pay for the same streaming service, an app renews annually on a card you rarely look at. This is a simple way to find all of them in about 30 minutes and cancel what you don't use.

## Step 1: Gather three months of statements

Pull up the last three months of statements for every account that might be paying for something:

- Every credit and debit card
- Your checking account
- PayPal, Venmo or other payment apps
- Your Apple, Google Play or Amazon account (subscriptions billed through them may show up only as "Apple" or "Google" on your card statement)

Three months matters because it catches monthly charges at least three times, and some quarterly ones too. Then do a separate search for annual charges: look back 12 months for anything that appears once a year.

## Step 2: Make a list

For every recurring charge, write down:

- What it's for (search the merchant name if you don't recognize it)
- How much and how often
- Which card or account it's on
- Whether anyone in the household actually uses it

Look out for **duplicates**: the same service paid for by two people, or a service you already get free through your phone plan, internet provider or credit card.

## Step 3: Find the hidden ones

Check these places, since their subscriptions are easy to miss on a statement:

- **iPhone:** Settings → your name → Subscriptions
- **Android:** Google Play Store → profile icon → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions
- **Amazon:** Your Account → Memberships & Subscriptions
- **PayPal:** Settings → Payments → Manage automatic payments

## Step 4: Cancel, then confirm

For each one you don't want:

1. Cancel through the same place you signed up (the app, the website or your app store account).
2. Take a screenshot or save the confirmation email.
3. Check your next statement to make sure the charge stopped.

Federal law (the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act) requires online subscriptions to give you a simple way to stop recurring charges, and a number of states have their own easy-cancellation laws. If a company makes it hard to cancel, keep a record of your attempts.

## Step 5: If they keep charging you

If you're charged after you cancelled:

- Contact the company with your cancellation confirmation and ask for a refund.
- If that doesn't work, dispute the charge with your card issuer. Under federal law you generally have 60 days from the statement date to dispute a billing error on a credit card.
- As a last resort, ask your bank about blocking the merchant or replacing the card.

## Step 6: Keep it from happening again

- Put free trial end dates in your calendar the day you sign up.
- Use one card for all subscriptions so they're easy to review.
- Repeat this review every six months.
