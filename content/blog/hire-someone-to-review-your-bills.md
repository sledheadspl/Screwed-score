# Should You Pay Someone to Review Your Bills? What a Bill Audit Covers

Most people know they're probably overpaying somewhere but never find the time to sit down and check. A bill audit is exactly that: someone goes through your bills, statements and contracts with you, line by line, and helps you decide what to dispute, cancel or renegotiate.

Here's what it involves, and how to tell whether it's worth paying for.

## What gets reviewed

A good review looks across everything you pay regularly, not just one bill:

- **Medical and dental bills**, compared against your insurance Explanation of Benefits
- **Phone, internet and TV**, especially expired promotions, equipment fees and add-ons
- **Bank and credit card statements**, for fees, interest and charges you don't recognize
- **Subscriptions**, including duplicates and annual renewals
- **Insurance** (auto, home, renters), for coverage that no longer fits and renewals that crept up
- **Contracts and estimates**, such as leases, contractor quotes and auto repair estimates

## What you should leave with

- A clear list of what to dispute, cancel or call about
- The words to use: what to say on the call or in the letter
- An idea of which items are worth your time and which aren't

## Is it worth paying for?

It usually is if:

- You have a large or confusing medical bill
- Your monthly bills have crept up and you don't know why
- You keep meaning to deal with it and haven't
- You'd simply rather have someone walk through it with you

It usually isn't if you have a single small bill and the time to call about it yourself.
