# Dental Bill Errors: What to Check Before You Pay

Dental bills are a common source of surprise charges. Insurance estimates given at the front desk often differ from what the plan actually pays, procedures get coded differently than you expected, and the "patient balance" can show up weeks later. Here's what to check.

## Get the right documents

You need two things:

1. **An itemized statement from the dental office.** It should list each procedure with the date, the procedure code (dental procedures use CDT codes, such as D1110 for an adult cleaning) and the fee.
2. **The Explanation of Benefits (EOB) from your dental insurer.** It shows what the office billed, what your plan allowed, what it paid and what it says you owe.

The patient balance on your bill should match the "patient responsibility" on your EOB. If it doesn't, ask why.

## Common dental billing problems

- **Billing above the allowed amount.** If your dentist is in your insurer's network, they've usually agreed to accept the plan's allowed amount. Being billed for the difference (balance billing) is generally not allowed for in-network providers.
- **Duplicate procedures.** The same X-ray or cleaning billed twice, or billed again inside the window your plan allows (for example, two cleanings in six months on a plan that covers one).
- **Different procedure than expected.** A deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) costs much more than a regular cleaning. If you were told you were getting a regular cleaning, check which code was billed.
- **Frequency limits and waiting periods.** Your plan may only cover certain procedures every few years or after a waiting period. If you weren't told, ask the office why it didn't check before treatment.
- **Missing pre-authorization.** For major work like crowns or implants, offices often send a pre-treatment estimate to the insurer. If they didn't and the claim was denied, ask whether they can submit one now or appeal.
- **Annual maximum reached.** Dental plans have yearly maximums. If you hit it, the rest falls to you. Check whether the date of service, or splitting treatment across two plan years, would have made a difference for future work.

## How to get it fixed

1. **Call the dental office first.** Billing errors are often fixed quickly once someone looks. Ask them to resubmit the claim with the correct code if needed.
2. **Call your insurer** if the EOB looks wrong or a claim was denied. Ask exactly why, and whether the office can resubmit with more information.
3. **Appeal a denial in writing** if needed. Your EOB or denial letter explains how, and the deadline.
4. **Ask about payment options** for the remaining balance: a discount for paying in full, or an interest-free payment plan.

Put disputes in writing and keep copies.

## Before your next treatment

- Ask for a written treatment plan with the procedure codes and costs.
- Ask the office to send a pre-treatment estimate to your insurer for anything major.
- Ask how much of your annual maximum is left.
